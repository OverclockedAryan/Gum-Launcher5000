# Ballistics model: launcher sizing and error budget

Generated by `tools/ballistics_sim.py`. Re-run it after weighing real pellets and chronographing the launcher; calibration shots replace every number here.

Model: two-dimensional point mass with quadratic drag, RK4 integration, origin at the muzzle. The robot measures the line of sight to the mouth (the camera rides on the turret) and the slant range, then raises the barrel by a ballistic offset above that line of sight.

## Assumptions

| Parameter | Value | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pellet mass | 1.40 g | typical pellet gum; weigh 30 of yours |
| Mean frontal area | 110 mm² | between flying flat (~70) and tumbling (~150) |
| Drag coefficient | 0.90 | blunt body, 0.8–1.2 plausible |
| Drag constant k | 0.0424 1/m | a = −k·|v|·v |
| Mouth above muzzle | 0.25 m (up to 0.45 m) | seated person, desk robot |
| Hit circle | 30 mm diameter at 1 m | brief |
| Aim point | 5 mm below mouth centre | lands on the tongue, further from the eyes |
| Eye zone lower edge | 45 mm above mouth centre | eyes sit ~65 mm above the mouth, minus margin; the robot uses live landmarks |
| Exit-energy cap | 40 mJ | conservative; see docs/research/safety-standards.md |

## Recommendation

**Build the launcher for 6.0 m/s nominal exit speed**: the lowest speed that stays under the 40 mJ cap, reaches a mouth 0.45 m up at 2 m below 40° elevation, and gives at least 95 % hits at 1 m with the target budget (margin over the 90 % requirement). The lowest qualifying speed is preferred because it arrives in the mouth most gently.

## Updating this model

1. Weigh 30 pellets on a 0.01 g scale and pass the mean as `--mass-g`; caliper them for `--area-mm2`.
2. Chronograph 30 shots: mean speed sets `--v0`, the standard deviation sets `--sigma-speed`.
3. Fit `--cd` so the model matches the measured drop at 1 m and 2 m, then re-run and commit the report.
